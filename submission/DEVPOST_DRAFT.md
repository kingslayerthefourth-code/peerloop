## Project name

PeerLoop

## Tagline

Turn what your club knows into the help everyone needs.

## Problem

A school club can have enough knowledge to help its members while still struggling to arrange useful exchanges. A student who can explain Python may need help with mathematics; the person who knows mathematics may need design help. A direct swap does not connect everyone.

This is the problem PeerLoop is intended to address. We have not yet interviewed users or measured its prevalence, and the final submission must not imply otherwise.

## Proposed solution — revise after verification

PeerLoop is planned as an organizer's tool that turns skill offers, help requests, and availability into suggested sessions. It will support individual help, reciprocal pairs, and three-person exchanges, explain each match, and retain unresolved requests. The organizer will be able to print the plan and save club data locally.

The planned audience is a small school club arranging free peer support. V1 deliberately uses one organizer's browser and does not synchronize or send messages to students.

## Technical approach — verify against the release

Planned stack: React, TypeScript, Vite, IndexedDB, and GitHub Pages. A deterministic matching engine will consider compatible subjects and time windows, prioritize fulfilled requests, and avoid assigning a member twice. Results are suggestions, not guaranteed optimal schedules or confirmed bookings.

English and Arabic interfaces and offline use after an initial visit are planned. No paid inference API is needed; Codex is being used as a development assistant.

## Limitations and next steps

The intended v1 uses a fixed subject list, a simple greedy matching method, and one organizer's device. It does not assess tutoring quality, synchronize a community, contact participants, or prove improved grades. Potential next steps include consent-based feedback from a real club and better scheduling, based on observed needs rather than invented demand.
